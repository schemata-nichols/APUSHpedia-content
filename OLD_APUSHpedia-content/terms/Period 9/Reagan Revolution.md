> The **Reagan Revolution** was the conservative political transformation associated with Ronald Reagan's presidency from 1981 to 1989, built on decades of opposition to the [[New Deal]], the [[Great Society]], social liberalism, taxation, regulation, and perceived American weakness abroad. Reagan joined business conservatives, anticommunists, white suburban voters, and the religious [[New Right]] while promoting tax cuts, [[Deregulation]], reduced domestic spending, military buildup, and traditional social values. For APUSH, the revolution shifted national debate toward markets and limited government, but it did not dismantle major entitlement programs, and expanded defense spending and tax reductions increased federal deficits.

## APUSH Significance

The Reagan Revolution reshaped the modern political order:

1. **Conservatism became nationally dominant.** Republicans connected economic, religious, racial, and [[Cold War]] concerns into a durable coalition.
2. **Tax and regulatory policy changed direction.** Government favored lower marginal rates, business investment, and reduced oversight.
3. **Organized labor weakened.** The [[PATCO Strike]] signaled a more aggressive climate for employers.
4. **Cold War pressure intensified before negotiation.** Military buildup accompanied later diplomacy with [[Mikhail Gorbachev]].
5. **The revolution had limits.** Social Security and Medicare survived, federal spending remained large, and government power expanded in defense and criminal policy.

The term describes a shift in political priorities, not the disappearance of the federal state. Reagan redirected government more than he simply reduced it.

## Conservative Foundations

Postwar conservatism developed through anticommunism, business resistance to regulation, opposition to civil-rights and cultural change, and western and suburban growth. Barry Goldwater's 1964 campaign lost decisively but helped organize activists and redefine Republican principles.

The [[Southern Strategy]] moved many white southern voters toward the [[Republican Party]]. Court decisions on school prayer, abortion, and rights, together with feminism and gay liberation, mobilized religious conservatives. The [[Moral Majority]] and similar organizations framed politics as a defense of family, faith, and national order.

Economic stagnation, inflation, the [[1973 Oil Crisis]], the Iran hostage crisis, and distrust after Vietnam and Watergate weakened confidence in liberal government. Reagan's communication skills joined these grievances to an optimistic promise of national renewal.

## Reaganomics and the Federal Government

[[Reaganomics]] drew on [[Supply-Side Economics]], arguing that lower tax rates would encourage investment, production, and growth. Congress enacted major tax cuts in 1981, followed later by tax reforms that lowered rates and broadened the tax base.

The administration reduced some domestic programs and weakened enforcement in environmental, labor, and consumer policy. Deregulation had begun earlier, but Reagan made it a central governing principle. Monetary policy under Federal Reserve chair Paul Volcker defeated inflation through high interest rates, contributing to a deep early recession before recovery.

Economic growth resumed, but inequality and homelessness increased, manufacturing employment continued to decline, and benefits were uneven. Tax cuts combined with defense spending produced large deficits, contradicting promises of balanced budgets.

## Labor, Race, and Social Policy

Reagan fired striking air traffic controllers in 1981 after their union violated federal law. The action accelerated a broader decline in union power and normalized permanent replacement of strikers in private industry.

The administration opposed or narrowed some affirmative-action and civil-rights enforcement efforts while rarely attacking popular programs such as Social Security. The crack epidemic and punitive criminal policies contributed to mass incarceration and unequal racial consequences.

Reagan used colorblind language and appeals to individual responsibility, but critics argued that attacks on welfare and federal intervention ignored structural inequality. The administration was also slow to respond to the AIDS crisis, intensifying distrust among gay communities and public-health advocates.

## Foreign Policy and the Cold War

Reagan increased defense spending, supported anticommunist forces abroad, and proposed the [[Strategic Defense Initiative]]. His administration aided authoritarian governments and insurgencies considered barriers to Soviet influence.

The [[Iran–Contra Affair]] revealed officials secretly selling arms to Iran and channeling funds to Contra rebels in Nicaragua despite congressional restrictions. The scandal raised questions about executive secrecy and accountability.

Relations shifted after Gorbachev introduced reforms. Reagan negotiated the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty, which eliminated a class of missiles. Military pressure mattered, but Soviet economic problems, internal reform, European movements, and diplomacy also contributed to the [[End of the Cold War]].

## Legacy

Reagan left office popular and helped make skepticism toward taxation, welfare, regulation, and federal bureaucracy central to national politics. Later Democrats also accommodated market-oriented policies, illustrating the breadth of the ideological shift.

Yet the federal government remained deeply involved in retirement, health care, defense, policing, and economic management. The Reagan Revolution's most durable achievement was therefore changing the terms of debate: public programs increasingly had to justify themselves against conservative assumptions about markets, personal responsibility, and centralized power.
