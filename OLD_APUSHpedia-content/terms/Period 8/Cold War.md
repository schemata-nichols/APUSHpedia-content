> The **Cold War** was the geopolitical, ideological, economic, and military rivalry between the United States and Soviet Union that emerged after [[World War II]] and lasted until the Soviet collapse in 1991. The superpowers avoided direct full-scale war but competed through alliances, nuclear arms, economic aid, propaganda, espionage, and conflicts in Europe, Asia, Latin America, Africa, and the Middle East. American leaders adopted [[Containment]] to resist communist expansion, while fears of subversion shaped politics at home. For APUSH, the Cold War connected foreign policy to federal spending, civil liberties, race, technology, and presidential power across nearly five decades.

## APUSH Significance

The Cold War reorganized American power and society:

1. **Containment became a durable strategy.** Different presidents used economic, diplomatic, covert, and military means against perceived communist expansion.
2. **Permanent alliances and military institutions expanded.** The United States rejected older reluctance toward peacetime commitments.
3. **Nuclear weapons changed strategy.** Deterrence reduced incentives for direct war while making confrontation potentially catastrophic.
4. **Domestic politics became anticommunist.** Loyalty programs and the [[Second Red Scare]] restricted dissent and damaged civil liberties.
5. **Global rivalry affected reform at home.** Civil-rights activists exposed the contradiction between democratic claims abroad and racism within the United States.

The Cold War was not a period without warfare. It was “cold” because the superpowers avoided fighting each other directly while proxy wars caused enormous destruction elsewhere.

## Origins and Containment

The wartime alliance masked deep conflict. Soviet leaders sought security after devastating invasions and established friendly communist governments in Eastern Europe. American leaders supported political self-determination, open markets, and institutions favorable to U.S. influence. Each side interpreted the other's moves as aggressive.

The [[Truman Doctrine]] promised aid to governments resisting communist pressure, initially Greece and Turkey. The [[Marshall Plan]] funded western European recovery, while the Soviet Union rejected participation and tightened control in its sphere. The Berlin blockade and [[Berlin Airlift]] divided Germany and hardened rival blocs.

The United States joined [[NATO]] in 1949, a major departure from avoiding permanent European alliances. The Soviet atomic test and [[Chinese Communist Revolution]] that year intensified American insecurity. [[NSC-68]] called for a major military buildup and globalized containment.

## Hot Wars and Global Competition

The [[Korean War]] began when North Korea invaded South Korea in 1950. A [[United Nations]] force led by the United States prevented southern defeat, advanced north, and then fought Chinese forces. The 1953 armistice restored a divided peninsula without a peace treaty.

In Southeast Asia, the [[Domino Theory]] encouraged support for anticommunist governments. Escalation in the [[Vietnam War]] produced a prolonged conflict that undermined public trust and demonstrated limits on American military power.

Elsewhere, the United States used aid, covert action, and intervention to support governments considered anticommunist, including authoritarian regimes. The CIA helped overthrow governments in Iran and Guatemala, showing that containment often took priority over democratic self-determination.

The [[Cuban Missile Crisis]] brought the superpowers close to nuclear war in 1962. Its resolution encouraged crisis-management mechanisms but did not end competition.

## Nuclear Arms, Technology, and the Economy

The arms race produced hydrogen bombs, intercontinental missiles, submarines, and doctrines of deterrence and mutually assured destruction. Civil-defense drills and fallout shelters brought nuclear anxiety into daily life.

The Soviet launch of Sputnik accelerated federal investment in science, mathematics, higher education, and space exploration. Defense spending supported aerospace, electronics, research universities, and growth in the [[Sun Belt]], strengthening the [[Military-Industrial Complex]].

Cold War spending contributed to the [[Postwar Economic Boom]], but resources devoted to defense competed with domestic needs. Technology developed for security also transformed civilian communications and industry.

## Domestic Anticommunism and Reform

Federal loyalty investigations, congressional hearings, blacklists, and [[McCarthyism]] equated dissent with disloyalty. Genuine espionage existed, but accusations often exceeded evidence and targeted labor activists, artists, gay federal employees, and political critics.

Cold War rhetoric influenced the [[African American Civil Rights Movement]]. [[Segregation]] damaged the nation's reputation among newly independent countries, giving federal officials an international reason to support limited change. At the same time, activists could be attacked as communist sympathizers.

Presidents expanded covert operations and military commitments, frequently acting without formal declarations of war. The Cold War therefore strengthened [[Executive Power]] and generated later demands for congressional oversight.

## Détente, Renewed Tension, and the End

During [[Détente]], the Nixon administration negotiated arms limits and opened relations with China while continuing geopolitical competition. The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979 helped end this relaxation.

Ronald Reagan increased defense spending, challenged Soviet influence, and negotiated with [[Mikhail Gorbachev]], whose reforms responded to economic stagnation and political pressure within the Soviet system. Eastern European communist governments fell in 1989, and the Soviet Union dissolved in 1991.

The [[End of the Cold War]] resulted from interacting forces rather than one policy alone. Its legacy included global alliances, nuclear arsenals, an enlarged national-security state, and recurring debate over whether American power should defend democracy, markets, strategic interests, or some combination.
