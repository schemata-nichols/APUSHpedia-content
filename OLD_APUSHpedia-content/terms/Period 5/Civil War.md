> The **Civil War** was fought from 1861 to 1865 between the United States and eleven seceded slave states organized as the Confederacy. [[Secession]] followed Abraham Lincoln's election after the [[Sectional Crisis]] made slavery's expansion the central national conflict. The Union initially fought to preserve the nation, but enslaved people's flight, military necessity, abolitionist pressure, and federal policy transformed emancipation into a war aim. Greater population, industry, finance, transportation, and naval power ultimately favored the Union, though Confederate resistance made victory costly. For APUSH, the war preserved the Union, destroyed slavery, expanded federal capacity, and created unresolved questions of citizenship and equality that defined [[Reconstruction]].

## APUSH Significance

The war transformed the Constitution and federal state:

1. **[[Slavery]] caused secession.** Confederate states left to protect the institution against perceived political containment.
2. **Union and emancipation became connected.** Ending slavery weakened the Confederacy and redefined national purpose.
3. **Mobilization expanded government.** Taxation, conscription, banking, land policy, and military administration increased federal reach.
4. **Black Americans shaped victory.** Enslaved people undermined Confederate labor and nearly 180,000 Black soldiers served the Union.
5. **Victory did not settle freedom's meaning.** The war ended slavery but left citizenship, voting, land, and protection for Reconstruction.

The Civil War was not initially a universal northern crusade for abolition. Its goals changed as military conflict and enslaved people's actions made slavery impossible to separate from Union victory.

## Secession and Opening War

Lincoln's victory in the [[Election of 1860]] prompted South Carolina and six other Deep South states to secede before his inauguration. They formed the Confederate States of America under Jefferson Davis. Four more states joined after fighting began.

Confederate forces attacked [[Fort Sumter]] in April 1861 after Lincoln attempted to resupply it. Lincoln called for troops, forcing Upper South states to choose sides. The [[Border States]] remained in the Union, making cautious policy toward slavery strategically important early in the war.

Both sides expected a shorter conflict. Confederate strategy sought survival, foreign recognition, and erosion of northern will; the Union had to restore authority across a vast region.

## Resources and Strategy

The Union possessed a larger population, most industry, extensive railroads, financial capacity, and naval power. The Confederacy fought defensively on familiar territory and initially had experienced officers, but it struggled to replace men and material.

The [[Anaconda Plan]] called for blockade, control of the Mississippi River, and pressure on the Confederate interior. Union strategy developed unevenly, while the Confederacy attempted offensives to influence northern politics and foreign opinion.

Both governments used conscription and taxation. Opposition included [[Copperheads]] in the North, desertion in the South, and the [[New York City Draft Riots]], which combined class resentment with racist violence.

## Emancipation and Black Military Service

Enslaved people fled to Union forces from the beginning. Commanders classified some as contraband, and Congress passed confiscation laws. Their movement forced federal officials to confront slavery as a military institution.

After the strategic Union success at the [[Battle of Antietam]], Lincoln issued the preliminary [[Emancipation Proclamation]]. The final order freed enslaved people in rebelling areas, not loyal border states, and authorized Black enlistment.

The proclamation changed the war's purpose, discouraged European support for the Confederacy, and recognized enslaved people as agents of Union victory. The [[Reconstruction Amendments|Thirteenth Amendment]] later abolished slavery nationally.

## Turning Points and Total War

In July 1863, Union victory at the [[Battle of Gettysburg]] ended Lee's major northern invasion, while the [[Siege of Vicksburg]] gave the Union control of the Mississippi. Together they shifted strategic momentum.

Ulysses S. Grant coordinated relentless campaigns against Confederate armies. William [[Tecumseh]] Sherman's capture of Atlanta and march through Georgia targeted railroads, supplies, and the southern capacity to wage war. This [[Total War|hard-war strategy]] distinguished between broad military infrastructure and unlimited violence but imposed severe civilian costs.

Lee surrendered to Grant at Appomattox Court House in April 1865. Other Confederate forces soon followed.

## Government and Society

Republicans enacted the [[Homestead Act]], [[Pacific Railway Act]], [[Morrill Land-Grant Act]], and National Banking Acts, advancing a free-labor development program. The federal government introduced national currency and the first federal income tax.

Women managed farms and businesses, organized aid, worked as nurses, and supported both war efforts. Death, disability, inflation, and destruction transformed households across both sections.

Lincoln suspended habeas corpus in some circumstances, producing enduring debate over [[Executive Power]] in wartime.

## Union, Emancipation, and Federal Power

The Union's material advantages mattered, but victory was not automatic. Political leadership, military adaptation, Black participation, and the ability to sustain public support converted resources into success.

The war was both preservation and revolution. It rejected secession and abolished slavery, but only Reconstruction could determine whether emancipation would bring equal citizenship and enforceable rights.
