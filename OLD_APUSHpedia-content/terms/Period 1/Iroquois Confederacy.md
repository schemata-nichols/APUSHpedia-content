> The **Iroquois Confederacy**, more accurately called the **Haudenosaunee Confederacy**, was an alliance originally joining the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, and Seneca nations under the Great Law of Peace; the Tuscarora joined later. Located in the Northeast, the confederacy coordinated diplomacy and security while member nations retained substantial autonomy. Clan mothers held important authority in selecting leaders, and consensus shaped council decisions. For APUSH, the Confederacy demonstrates sophisticated Indigenous governance before European colonization and explains how Native diplomacy influenced the balance among European empires and colonies.

## APUSH Significance

Haudenosaunee oral tradition places the Confederacy's formation before sustained European contact, although historians debate its precise date. The alliance reduced conflict among its member nations and created a council of representatives, or sachems, chosen through clan structures. Its political system was neither a European-style nation-state nor a simple military alliance. It combined unity in external affairs with local sovereignty and depended on kinship, diplomacy, and consensus, making it evidence of the varied political institutions within [[Native American Societies Before European Contact]].

## Diplomacy and Division

As Dutch, French, and British colonizers competed for land and trade, Haudenosaunee leaders used commerce, war, neutrality, and treaty diplomacy to protect their interests. Their strategic position made the Confederacy a major power in northeastern affairs. During the [[American Revolution]], member nations divided over whether to support Britain, the revolutionaries, or neutrality, and the war damaged the alliance and accelerated land loss. The Confederacy survived, however, and Haudenosaunee governments continue to assert sovereignty. Its history centers Native political choices rather than treating Indigenous peoples only as auxiliaries to European conflict.

## The Great Law and Confederated Power

The Great Law of Peace established a council in which the original five nations worked through appointed chiefs while preserving their own councils and internal affairs. Clan mothers selected and could remove male sachems, connecting political authority to matrilineal clan relationships. Council decisions emphasized consensus. This arrangement made united diplomacy possible without creating a centralized state modeled on European monarchy.

The Confederacy's founding chronology remains debated because Haudenosaunee oral traditions and archaeological or documentary methods organize evidence differently. The uncertainty should not be used to dismiss the tradition or assign a falsely precise European date. What is clear is that the league preceded its central role in seventeenth- and eighteenth-century colonial diplomacy and provided a durable framework for peace among its members.

Claims that the Confederacy directly supplied a model for the United States Constitution are also debated. American founders knew of Indigenous diplomacy and federal arrangements, but broad similarity does not establish a simple line of borrowing. The firmer APUSH point is that colonists and founders interacted with a sophisticated confederated government, contradicting claims that representative or federal political reasoning existed only in Europe.

## Empire, Revolution, and Sovereignty

The Haudenosaunee occupied a strategic position between French and British colonies and controlled access to significant trade and diplomatic networks. Through the Covenant Chain and other relationships, leaders managed alliances while attempting to preserve autonomy. European goods and weapons altered power, and epidemic disease caused losses, but Native leaders pursued their own objectives rather than serving merely as proxies.

The [[French and Indian War]] changed the imperial balance by removing France as a North American competitor and increasing British-colonial pressure on Native land. During the American Revolution, the Oneida and Tuscarora generally supported the Americans while Mohawk, Seneca, Cayuga, and Onondaga communities more often sided with Britain or divided internally. The resulting warfare devastated towns and crops, especially during the Sullivan-Clinton campaign, and the peace settlement ignored Native allies and adversaries alike.

After independence, land cessions and settlement fragmented Haudenosaunee territory across the United States and Canada. Communities nevertheless preserved the Confederacy, ceremonies, and national governments. Its continued existence makes sovereignty—not disappearance—the proper endpoint of the history.
