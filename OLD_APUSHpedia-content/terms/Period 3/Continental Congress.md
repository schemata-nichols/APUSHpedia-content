> The **Continental Congress** refers to the intercolonial assemblies that coordinated resistance and then governed the revolutionary cause. The First Continental Congress met in 1774 to answer the Coercive Acts through petitions and economic pressure; the Second convened in 1775 after fighting began and eventually directed war, declared independence, sought foreign aid, and drafted the [[Articles of Confederation]]. Delegates represented colonies with distinct interests and uncertain authority. For APUSH, Congress shows how protest institutions became a national government through wartime necessity before Americans had settled the constitutional balance between state sovereignty and union.

## APUSH Significance

The First Congress endorsed colonial rights and created the Continental Association to enforce a boycott of British goods, but it did not demand independence. After [[Lexington and Concord]], the Second Congress organized the Continental Army and selected [[George Washington]] as commander while still sending the Olive Branch Petition to the king. The shift toward independence reflected continuing warfare, Britain's rejection of reconciliation, and arguments such as [[Common Sense]], not a unanimous plan present from the beginning.

## Governing a Revolution

Congress issued paper money, managed diplomacy, requested troops and funds from states, and approved the [[Declaration of Independence]]. Yet it lacked a reliable independent tax base and depended heavily on state cooperation. Those weaknesses continued under the Articles of Confederation and later shaped calls for the [[Constitutional Convention]]. The Continental Congress was therefore both effective and limited: it created enough coordination to sustain revolution and international diplomacy, but its reliance on states revealed the central problem of constructing national authority from formerly separate colonies.

## The First Continental Congress

Delegates from twelve colonies met in Philadelphia in 1774 after Parliament enacted the Coercive Acts; Georgia did not participate. They differed over how aggressively to confront Britain. Some still hoped for reconciliation, while others believed only coordinated resistance could defend colonial rights. The congress had no established constitutional authority and depended on colonies and local committees to accept its recommendations.

Delegates issued a Declaration and Resolves asserting colonial rights and created the Continental Association, a program of nonimportation, nonconsumption, and eventual nonexportation. Local committees monitored compliance, extending political organization into communities and pressuring dissenters. Economic resistance thus became a form of parallel authority before the colonies declared independence.

## The Second Continental Congress

The Second Congress convened in May 1775 after fighting at Lexington and Concord. It adopted the militia surrounding Boston as the Continental Army and appointed George Washington commander. At the same time, the [[Olive Branch Petition]] showed that many delegates still hoped to restore peace without abandoning their claims. The king's rejection and continuing war weakened that position.

In 1776, arguments such as [[Common Sense]] and instructions from colonial governments pushed delegates toward independence. Congress approved the [[Declaration of Independence]], sought the [[French Alliance]], issued paper currency, coordinated military supply, and conducted diplomacy. These were national functions, but Congress lacked a reliable taxing power or executive administration and depended heavily on state cooperation.

## From Revolution to Confederation

The Congress governed through committees, resolutions, personal networks, and persuasion. It faced inflation, shortages, regional rivalry, and disagreement over western land. Its weaknesses did not mean it accomplished nothing: sustaining an army and foreign alliance across years of war was an extraordinary act of political coordination.

Delegates drafted the [[Articles of Confederation]], which states completed ratifying in 1781. The Articles formalized a union that preserved extensive state sovereignty and continued congressional responsibility for war and diplomacy. The Continental Congress should therefore be distinguished from the later federal Congress created by the Constitution. It was an improvised revolutionary institution that helped separate colonies become states and then confronted the difficulty of making those states act as one nation.
