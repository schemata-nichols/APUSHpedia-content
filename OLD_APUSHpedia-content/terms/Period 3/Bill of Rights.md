> The **Bill of Rights** is the first ten amendments to the [[United States Constitution]], ratified in 1791. It protects freedoms including religion, speech, press, assembly, and petition; establishes safeguards for accused people and property; and reserves undelegated powers to the states or people. The amendments resulted from the [[Ratification Debate]], in which [[Anti-Federalists]] demanded explicit limits on national authority and [[Federalists]] promised changes to secure approval. For APUSH, the Bill of Rights made civil liberties central to constitutional legitimacy, but its protections initially restrained only the federal government and were enforced unevenly.

## APUSH Significance

James Madison drafted proposed amendments from state recommendations, and Congress sent twelve to the states; ten were ratified in 1791. Some proposals clarified individual freedoms, while the Ninth and Tenth Amendments addressed fears that listing rights might narrow popular sovereignty or erase state powers. The amendments did not abolish slavery, establish universal suffrage, or resolve who counted as a full citizen, demonstrating the limits of the founding settlement.

## Expanding Application

The Supreme Court initially held that the Bill of Rights did not apply to state governments. After the Civil War, the Fourteenth Amendment provided the basis for courts to apply most of its guarantees to states through incorporation, making the document far more important in everyday civil-rights disputes. Interpretation has continued to change through cases involving expression, religion, policing, privacy, and firearms. The Bill of Rights should therefore be understood as both an eighteenth-century political compromise and a framework whose practical reach expanded through later amendments, litigation, federal enforcement, and [[Social Movements]].
