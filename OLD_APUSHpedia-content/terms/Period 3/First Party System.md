> The **First Party System** was the political competition between the Federalist and Democratic-Republican parties from the 1790s into the 1810s. It developed despite widespread founding-era fears that organized factions would sacrifice the public good. Disputes over [[Alexander Hamilton|Hamilton's]] financial program, [[Constitutional Interpretation]], the French Revolution, relations with Britain, and the power of the federal government created durable coalitions. For APUSH, the system transformed disagreement into organized electoral opposition and helped normalize peaceful transfers of power, even as partisan newspapers, repression, and narrow suffrage limited democratic participation.

## APUSH Significance

Federalists favored Hamiltonian finance, commercial development, closer British relations, and energetic national authority. Democratic-Republicans led by [[Thomas Jefferson]] and James Madison defended a more agrarian republic, sympathized more strongly with France, and warned against centralized power. Both sides built newspapers, congressional networks, local organizations, and presidential tickets. Their conflict showed that the Constitution did not settle policy: competing interpretations and social interests had to be organized through politics.

## Conflict and Decline

The [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] and [[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]] made civil liberties and federalism partisan issues. Jefferson's victory in 1800 transferred executive power peacefully, proving opposition could replace an administration without overthrowing the constitutional order. Federalists declined after opposing the [[War of 1812]] and appearing sectional at the Hartford Convention, leading to the [[Era of Good Feelings]]. The First Party System differs from the later [[Second Party System]], but it established a lasting pattern in which parties mobilized voters, interpreted the Constitution, and linked local interests to national government.
