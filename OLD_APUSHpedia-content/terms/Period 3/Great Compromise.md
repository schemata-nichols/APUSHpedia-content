> The **Great Compromise**, or Connecticut Compromise, was the 1787 agreement at the [[Constitutional Convention]] that created a bicameral Congress with representation based on population in the House and equal representation for each state in the Senate. It resolved a major dispute between large-state supporters of the Virginia Plan and small-state supporters of the New Jersey Plan. For APUSH, the compromise made continued constitution-writing possible and embodied a lasting tension in American government: national institutions represent both individual people and states, producing unequal voting power in the Senate by design.

## APUSH Significance

Under the [[Articles of Confederation]], each state had one vote regardless of population. Large-state delegates wanted representation to reflect population and financial contribution, while small states feared domination and threatened to reject a new union. The compromise gave the House proportional representation and the power to originate revenue bills, while every state received two senators selected by state legislatures under the original Constitution. This arrangement mixed national and federal principles rather than choosing one completely.

## Representation and Consequences

The compromise addressed state size, not slavery, although population-based House seats immediately raised the separate issue resolved through the [[Three-Fifths Compromise]]. Equal state representation protected small states and helped secure ratification, but it also meant citizens of less populous states received greater influence in the Senate. The arrangement later shaped sectional power because admission of new free and slave states affected the chamber's balance. The Great Compromise is therefore more than a successful bargain: it created a structural feature that continues to shape legislation, appointments, treaties, and presidential removal trials.
