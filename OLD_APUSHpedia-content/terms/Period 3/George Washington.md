> **George Washington** was commander in chief of the Continental Army during the [[American Revolution]], presiding officer of the [[Constitutional Convention]], and first president of the United States from 1789 to 1797. His military persistence and willingness to surrender command strengthened civilian authority, while his presidency established precedents for the executive branch. Washington supported a stronger Union and national fiscal program but warned against permanent alliances and destructive party conflict. For APUSH, his leadership helped make new institutions credible, yet his land speculation, slaveholding, and policies toward Native nations reveal the exclusions within the founding republic.

## APUSH Significance

Washington kept the Continental Army together through shortages and defeats and resigned his commission after victory, reinforcing republican fears of military dictatorship. As president, he formed a cabinet, asserted federal authority during the Whiskey Rebellion, accepted [[Alexander Hamilton|Hamilton's]] economic program, and declared neutrality in the European war. These choices gave practical meaning to constitutional powers that existed only on paper and demonstrated that the national government could enforce law.

## Precedent and Contradiction

Washington's voluntary retirement after two terms created an important precedent for peaceful transfer, while his Farewell Address urged national unity and caution toward enduring foreign commitments. Nevertheless, the [[First Party System]] developed inside his administration as Hamilton and [[Thomas Jefferson]] disagreed over finance, constitutional interpretation, and foreign affairs. Washington enslaved more than one hundred people at Mount Vernon and signed the Fugitive Slave Act of 1793. His legacy therefore combines republican restraint and institution-building with participation in slavery and expansion onto Native land.
