> The **American Revolution** was the political and military struggle through which thirteen British colonies declared independence and created the United States between the 1760s and 1783. It grew from conflict over Parliament's authority, taxation, western policy, and colonial self-government after the [[French and Indian War]]. Patriots mobilized natural-rights and republican arguments, while Loyalists, enslaved people, Native nations, women, and foreign powers pursued different interests. American victory depended on sustained local organization, British strategic difficulties, and the [[French Alliance]]. For APUSH, the Revolution created republican institutions and expansive ideals while leaving slavery, Native dispossession, women's political exclusion, and economic hierarchy substantially intact.

## APUSH Significance

The Revolution combined ideological change with a war for state power:

1. **Imperial reform produced constitutional resistance.** Colonists disputed Parliament's right to tax and govern them without their elected consent.
2. **Popular organization widened politics.** Boycotts, committees, pamphlets, congresses, and militias mobilized people outside ordinary institutions.
3. **Independence required foreign assistance.** French money, weapons, troops, and naval power were essential to victory.
4. **Revolutionary ideals encouraged later claims.** Abolitionists, women, and other groups used natural rights against exclusion.
5. **Freedom remained unequal.** The new republic preserved slavery and expanded onto Native lands.

The Revolution differs from the Revolutionary War. The war was the military conflict; the Revolution also included ideological, political, and social transformations before and after fighting.

## Imperial Crisis

Britain's postwar debt and new territory encouraged tighter administration. The [[Proclamation of 1763]] restricted western settlement, while the [[Sugar Act]] strengthened customs enforcement. The [[Stamp Act]] imposed a direct internal tax and provoked claims of [[Taxation Without Representation]].

Colonists used petitions, nonimportation, and crowd action. The Stamp Act Congress coordinated protest, and the [[Sons of Liberty]] pressured officials. Parliament repealed the tax but issued the Declaratory Act, asserting full legislative authority.

Later Townshend duties, the [[Boston Massacre]], and the [[Tea Act]] sustained resistance. After the [[Boston Tea Party]], the [[Coercive Acts]] closed Boston's port and altered Massachusetts government, persuading many colonists that local liberty faced a general threat.

## Independence

The First [[Continental Congress]] coordinated economic resistance. Fighting began at [[Lexington and Concord]] in April 1775, and the Second Continental Congress created the Continental Army under [[George Washington]]. Many colonists still hoped for reconciliation.

Thomas Paine's [[Common Sense]] attacked monarchy and made independence accessible to a broad audience. The [[Declaration of Independence]] then grounded separation in natural rights, consent, and a list of grievances against George III.

Colonists divided. [[Patriots and Loyalists]] existed in every region, while many people tried to remain neutral. Political allegiance reflected family, religion, location, economic interest, and judgments about security.

## War and Alliance

The British possessed a professional army, navy, and greater resources. Americans benefited from distance, local support, militia, and the ability to survive without conquering Britain. Washington preserved the Continental Army despite defeats and hardship at [[Valley Forge]].

The American victory at the [[Battle of Saratoga]] persuaded France to enter openly. French aid turned a colonial rebellion into an international war and stretched British resources. Spanish and Dutch involvement also complicated British strategy.

At [[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]] in 1781, American and French forces trapped Cornwallis while the French navy blocked escape. The [[Treaty of Paris of 1783]] recognized independence and granted the United States extensive western claims.

## Society and Uneven Freedom

Women maintained farms and businesses, supplied armies, participated in boycotts, and sometimes accompanied forces. [[Republican Motherhood]] later assigned women responsibility for educating virtuous citizens, expanding educational arguments without granting equal political rights.

Thousands of enslaved people pursued freedom by escaping to British or American lines. Northern states adopted gradual emancipation, but southern slavery survived. The contradiction between natural rights and bondage became more visible without being resolved.

Many Native nations allied with Britain because American settlement threatened their land. The peace treaty transferred western claims without Native consent, and the United States intensified expansion after victory.

## Political Consequences

States wrote constitutions and replaced royal authority with republican government. Voting expanded for some white men, though property restrictions remained. Religious establishment weakened in several places.

The national government under the [[Articles of Confederation]] reflected fear of centralized power. Its weakness later produced the [[Constitutional Convention]] and a new debate over how much authority republican government required.

## Independence, Revolution, and Its Limits

The Revolution was radical in rejecting monarchy and grounding government in popular authority. It was limited in the people permitted to exercise that authority. Both judgments are necessary.

Its most enduring consequence was an argumentative tradition: groups excluded from full citizenship could use the Revolution's own principles to demand change, even when founders had not intended those principles to apply equally.
