> **Common Sense** was Thomas Paine's widely circulated pamphlet, published in January 1776, that urged the American colonies to declare independence and establish republican government. Written in direct language for a broad audience, it attacked monarchy and hereditary rule, argued that reconciliation with Britain was neither desirable nor realistic, and presented continental independence as an opportunity. For APUSH, the pamphlet did not create revolutionary sentiment by itself; it gave accessible form to arguments already emerging from war and imperial crisis and helped move public debate from defense of colonial rights toward separation and [[Republicanism]].

## APUSH Significance

Paine rejected the idea that the British constitution uniquely protected liberty and portrayed monarchy as both irrational and dangerous. He argued that a large continent should not be ruled by a distant island and that independence would permit foreign alliances and freer commerce. Its inexpensive format, forceful prose, and rapid circulation brought constitutional debate beyond elite assemblies. The pamphlet appeared after fighting had begun but before the [[Declaration of Independence]], making it especially effective in framing separation as practical and morally justified.

## Persuasion and Limits

Not every colonist accepted Paine's conclusions; [[Patriots and Loyalists]] continued to disagree, and independence remained risky. His republican language attacked inherited political hierarchy without necessarily requiring equal political or social rights for women, enslaved people, or Native peoples. *Common Sense* is therefore valuable evidence of ideological change and popular print culture, not proof that one text caused independence. It shows how political writing can accelerate a movement when institutions, military conflict, and public experience have already made new arguments plausible.
