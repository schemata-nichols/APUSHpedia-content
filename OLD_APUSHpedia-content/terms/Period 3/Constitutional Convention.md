> The **Constitutional Convention** was the meeting of delegates from twelve states in Philadelphia from May to September 1787 that produced the [[United States Constitution]]. Called to revise the [[Articles of Confederation]], the delegates instead designed a new national government with legislative, executive, and judicial branches, divided powers, and authority operating directly on individuals. Bargaining over representation, slavery, commerce, and federalism shaped the document. For APUSH, the Convention joined innovative constitutional design to political compromise, especially compromises that protected slavery, and transferred the final decision to a contested [[Ratification Debate]].

## APUSH Significance

Delegates agreed that the Confederation lacked adequate taxing, commercial, and enforcement powers but differed over how states and people should be represented. The Virginia Plan favored population-based representation, while the New Jersey Plan defended equal state voting. The [[Great Compromise]] created a bicameral Congress, and the [[Three-Fifths Compromise]] increased slave-state representation without recognizing enslaved people's rights. Secrecy encouraged candid bargaining but also fueled later suspicion of elite intentions.

## Design and Limits

The Constitution divided authority through [[Federalism]], separation of powers, checks and balances, and an Electoral College. It strengthened national capacity while placing the House closest to direct popular election; senators and the president were selected indirectly under the original design. The Convention did not create a complete democracy, and its delegates were not authorized explicitly to replace the Articles. Its achievement therefore depended on ratification by special state conventions. The resulting system balanced competing interests without resolving conflicts over sovereignty, slavery, representation, or the scope of national power.
