> **Alexander Hamilton** was a Revolutionary officer, leading [[Federalists|Federalist]], principal author of many [[Federalist Papers]], and the first secretary of the treasury. He believed that public credit, manufacturing, commerce, and energetic national authority were essential to the survival of the republic. His financial program funded federal debt, assumed state debts, established the [[First Bank of the United States]], and supported excise taxes and industry. For APUSH, Hamilton represents a broad interpretation of the Constitution and state-supported capitalist development, while opposition to his program helped create the [[First Party System]].

## APUSH Significance

Hamilton sought to make federal securities reliable, tie wealthy creditors to the national government, and create stable sources of revenue. Assumption of state debts produced a bargain locating the permanent capital on the Potomac, while the Bank stored funds and expanded credit. In defending it, Hamilton argued that the Necessary and Proper Clause authorized implied powers needed to execute enumerated responsibilities. [[Thomas Jefferson]] rejected that reasoning and feared concentrated financial and political power.

## National Power and Division

Hamilton favored close commercial relations with Britain and supported forceful enforcement of the whiskey excise, reinforcing his vision of capable government. His *Report on Manufactures* proposed encouragement for economic diversification, though Congress adopted little of it immediately. Critics portrayed his policies as privileges for speculators and an imitation of British corruption; supporters considered them foundations for credit and growth. Hamilton did not simply favor “big government” in every sphere. He favored national power directed toward finance, commerce, military security, and elite-led stability, a program that permanently shaped debates over [[Capitalism]] and [[Constitutional Interpretation]].
