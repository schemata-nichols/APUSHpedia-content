> **Thomas Jefferson** drafted the [[Declaration of Independence]], served as the first secretary of state, led the Democratic-Republican opposition, and was president from 1801 to 1809. He celebrated [[Natural Rights]], agrarian independence, religious liberty, and limited national power, yet his policies often required pragmatic departures from those principles. The [[Louisiana Purchase]] doubled U.S. territory, while the Embargo Act expanded federal economic coercion. For APUSH, Jefferson embodies a central founding contradiction: he articulated universal equality while enslaving people and supporting a republic whose expansion threatened Native sovereignty.

## APUSH Significance

In [[George Washington|Washington's]] cabinet, Jefferson opposed [[Alexander Hamilton|Hamilton's]] national bank and broad use of implied powers, fearing that finance and centralized authority would reproduce European inequality. His allies formed the Democratic-Republican Party, contributing to the [[First Party System]]. The election of 1800 produced a peaceful transfer of power between parties, an important test of the constitutional system, even though voting and officeholding remained narrowly restricted.

## Principle and Power

As president, Jefferson reduced some taxes and military expenditures but purchased Louisiana despite uncertainty that the Constitution authorized acquiring territory. Expansion advanced his vision of an agrarian republic while intensifying pressure on Native land and raising the future of slavery in western territories. Jefferson's household and wealth depended on enslaved labor, and DNA and documentary evidence establish that he fathered children with Sally Hemings, whom he enslaved. His career is therefore analytically important not because ideals or hypocrisy alone define him, but because national liberty and expansion developed through racial and imperial inequalities he helped sustain.
