> **Shays' Rebellion** was an armed uprising in western Massachusetts in 1786–1787 by indebted farmers and other protesters who opposed high taxes, court foreclosures, and state economic policies. Led in part by Revolutionary War veteran Daniel Shays, insurgents closed courts and attempted to seize the federal arsenal at Springfield before a privately funded state force defeated them. For APUSH, the rebellion exposed class tensions after the [[American Revolution]] and became powerful evidence for advocates of a stronger national government, although it did not alone cause the [[Constitutional Convention]].

## APUSH Significance

Postwar depression, scarce hard currency, taxes, and debt collection placed heavy pressure on rural households. Protesters used tactics with precedents in colonial resistance, arguing that government had ignored their grievances. Massachusetts leaders, creditors, and merchants viewed the court closures as threats to property and republican order. Because the [[Articles of Confederation]] government lacked money and a dependable force, it could offer little assistance, while the state raised an army financed by private contributors.

## Competing Meanings of Liberty

Nationalists cited the uprising as proof that the Confederation could not protect property, maintain order, or respond to domestic crisis. Others regarded the farmers' grievances as legitimate and warned that suppressing protest could betray revolutionary principles. The event therefore posed a larger question: did republican liberty require stronger authority, or did concentrated authority threaten ordinary citizens? Shays' Rebellion accelerated existing efforts to revise the national system and shaped the atmosphere of the [[Ratification Debate]], but long-standing fiscal, commercial, and diplomatic weaknesses had already prompted calls for reform.
