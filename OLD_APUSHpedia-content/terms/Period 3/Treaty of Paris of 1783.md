> The **Treaty of Paris of 1783** formally ended the [[American Revolution]] between the United States and Great Britain. Britain recognized American independence and accepted boundaries extending west to the Mississippi River, while the United States agreed to recommend restitution of Loyalist property and prevent obstacles to collecting legitimate British debts. Spain and France concluded related settlements separately. For APUSH, the treaty gave the new nation extraordinary territorial claims but left major issues unresolved: Native nations were excluded from negotiations, British forces retained frontier posts, and weak national authority complicated compliance under the [[Articles of Confederation]].

## APUSH Significance

American negotiators [[Benjamin Franklin]], John Adams, and John Jay dealt directly with Britain despite instructions emphasizing consultation with France. The resulting boundaries reached from the Atlantic to the Mississippi and from the Great Lakes to Spanish Florida, giving the United States control over lands far larger than the settled states. Fishing rights off parts of British North America were also protected. These gains reflected British hopes for renewed commerce and a stable peace as well as American military and diplomatic success.

## Peace and Unsettled Sovereignty

The treaty treated western territory as something European-descended governments could transfer, ignoring Native peoples who lived there and had not surrendered sovereignty. Settler expansion therefore produced continuing conflict. Britain cited unpaid debts and Loyalist mistreatment when delaying evacuation of frontier forts, while Congress lacked power to compel states to honor treaty obligations. The agreement achieved international recognition without guaranteeing effective control. It consequently links revolutionary victory to the challenges of national governance, diplomacy, and [[Westward Expansion]] that shaped the 1780s and the movement for a stronger Constitution.
