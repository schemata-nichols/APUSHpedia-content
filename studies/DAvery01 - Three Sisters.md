---
title: "The Three Sisters: Native American Culture and Agriculture"
author: Drew Avery
---
Intercropping, or “companion planting,” of what the Native Americans call the Three Sisters involves growing corn, beans, and squash together in the same field so they can support each other. The crops have an interconnectedness and grow together so well because they protect and nourish each other. This integration of crops can be associated with the bond that three sisters would share in a family-setting, hence these crops earning the name “the Three Sisters.”

Culturally and spiritually, the Three Sisters played an important role in the lives of Native Americans. Several different stories and myths have been handed down through the generations about the origin of the Three Sisters. For example, the Haudenosaunee tale is based on the belief that three women who reunited discovered that collectively they were stronger together when they cooperated with each other. 

Agriculturally, the Native Americans began by planting corn in the field. Several weeks later, they planted beans. The corn stalks provided a pole for the beans to climb and grow, and the beans also contributed to the nutrients in the soil as a fertilizer. Finally, squash was planted between the rows of corn which provided shade to help keep in moisture for the crops and also aided in preventing the growth of weeds. These three crops were able to thrive when grown together and even provided a surplus of food for each family. 

As for nutrition, the Three Sisters were an essential part of the Native Americans’ diet. Corns, beans, and squash when cooked together with a protein (often fish, deer or bear), provided a completely balanced diet with high nutritional value for the Native Americans. In the 19th century, Native American tribes were displaced from their land by European settlers, which pushed the Native Americans on to land not suitable for growing crops. So they struggled to produce an ample amount of corns, beans and squash. Therefore, tribes had to rely on other sources of food, including processed foods, such as white flour and sugar, which led to significant health problems.
