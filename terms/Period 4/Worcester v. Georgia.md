## Submission by Doga Vanwesenbeeck

> Supreme court case where John Marshall rules in favor of Cherokee sovereignty.

### Section 1

Worcester and other missionaries were prohibited from staying on Cherokee land due to a new Georgia law; when they refused, they were sentenced to prison, sparking this court case. The supreme court under John Marshall ruled in favor of Cherokee sovereignty, stating that the United States could not make decisions on behalf of the Cherokee nation.

### Section 2

Important Effects to Know for the Exam:
Andrew Jackson ignored the court's decision (effectively nullifying it). His actions demonstrate a lack of consistency: during the Nullification Crisis he had used federal power to stop South Carolina from refusing tariffs; now he did not take a federal stance and instead encouraged Georgia to settle into and displace the Cherokee Indians. This decision is key to understanding how the Indian Removal Act was carried out and how the Trail of Tears came to be. The surviving displaced Cherokees were relocated to present day Oklahoma.

## Submission by Mitram Sinha

> Worcester v. Georgia was a U.S. Supreme Court case in which it was ruled that states did not have power to regulate activity in Native American Land.

### Section 1

The conflict of Worcester v. Georgia involved Samuel A. Worcester, a white missionary who had been invited by the Cherokee people to live on their land. Worcester lobbied for Cherokee protections against Georgia, who had wanted to enforce state laws on the Cherokee even though they were a sovereign nation. The Supreme Court sided with Worcester, deeming the actions of Georgia unconstitutional. Despite this ruling, Georgia, and ultimately President Andrew Jackson, ignored the decision, and went on to force the Cherokee off their land through the Trail of Tears.
