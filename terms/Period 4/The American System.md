> The American System, pushed by Henry Clay, was an economic policy that favored tariffs, a national bank, and subsidies. Ultimately ending due to an executive veto from president Andrew Jackson.

## Section 1

The American System was a policy devised by Henry Clay that focused on three mutually beneficial pillars: (1) the instatement of protective tariffs to promote American industry, (2) a national bank, and (3) improvement of roads and canals; it drew in part on the economic ideas of Alexander Hamilton. Clay founded the Whig Party, a party that opposed Andrew Jackson's executive overreach, in 1833 and his first speech outlining the American System was delivered one year prior. In this speech, he states his conviction concerning the necessity of economic intervention and defends that the policy will not be detrimental to the South. Southern senator John C. Calhoun of South Carolina was one of the main opposers of the system.

## Section 2

Ultimately, the system was vetoed in full by Jackson with the Maysville Road veto of 1830 and the Bank Veto of 1830. Jackson and his fellow democrats argued Congress lacked the power to establish national projects and it would infringe on states’ rights to govern themselves. Jackson additionally criticized the Second Bank, stating it appealed to the interests of the wealthy and not of the people.
