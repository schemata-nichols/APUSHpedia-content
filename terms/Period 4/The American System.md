## Section 1

The American System was a policy devised by Henry Clay that focused on three mutually beneficial pillars: (1) the instatement of protective tariffs to promote American industry, (2) a national bank, and (3) improvement of roads and canals; it drew in part on the economic ideas of Alexander Hamilton. Clay founded the Whig Party, a party that opposed Andrew Jackson's executive overreach, in 1833 and his first speech outlining the American System was delivered one year prior. In this speech, he states his conviction concerning the necessity of economic intervention and defends that the policy will not be detrimental to the South. Southern senator John C. Calhoun of South Carolina was one of the main opposers of the system. Ultimately, the system succeeded only partially. The Tariff of 1816 and the charter for the Second Bank of the United States made the future of the American System look promising, but Jacksonian America would soon render Clay's ideas impossible, especially with "King Cotton" in the South putting pressure on Congress. However, the System made a lasting impact today, funding canals like the Erie Canal.
