> Lyman Beecher (1775-1863) was an influential American Presbyterian minister and revivalist during the Second Great Awakening. He promoted religious revival and moral reform, especially the temperance movement. He was also the father of prominent abolitionists Harriet Beecher Stowe and Henry Ward Beecher.

## Section 1

Lyman Beecher was an influential Presbyterian minister, religious revivalist, and social reformer during the Second Great Awakening. He became known for powerful sermons that encouraged Americans to strengthen their religious beliefs and improve society. He was a big supporter of the temperance movement, delivering sermons against alcohol consumption that helped spread the movement nationally. His views on religious revival and theology sparked controversy, and he was tried for heresy but acquitted. His life reflects the connection between religion, social reform, and westward expansion in 19th century America.
