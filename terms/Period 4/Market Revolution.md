> The Market Revolution was a period of economic transformation in the United States during the early 19th century. It shifted the economy from primarily local, subsistence-based farming toward a national market system connected through improved transportation, new technology, and factory production.

## Section 1

The Market Revolution transformed the American economy during the early 19th century. Improvements in transportation, including the Erie Canal, steamboats, and railroads, allowed goods to travel faster and cheaper. New technologies, such as the cotton gin, increased production, while textile mills and factories expanded manufacturing, especially in the North. Farmers increasingly grew crops to sell in national markets rather than only for their own use. The North became more industrialized, the South expanded cotton production using enslaved labor, and the West supplied agricultural goods. These changes encouraged economic growth and urbanization but also increased economic inequality and strengthened slavery in the South. The Market Revolution is important to APUSH because it reshaped American society and regional economies, contributing to growing sectional differences during Period 4 (1800–1848).
