> Impressment was a military practice used by the British Navy to kidnap American merchants and sailors and force them to join their military. This was also called “the press gang” or “the press”. Britain started using this tactic to satisfy the demand for sailors in the Napoleonic Wars because voluntary enlistment couldn’t satisfy their desired military size. The practice of forced recruitment became especially feared and angered many Americans, especially when Britain excused it by saying that being a "British subject” took precedence over being an American citizen.

## Section 1

Impressment first led to the embargo act, a policy implemented by the United States Congress as a general trade embargo on foreign nations in an effort to boycott British impressment and to hopefully decrease it. Unfortunately for congress, it did not have the desired effect on Britain, and many sailors ignored it and both trade and impressment continued. Eventually this led to the war of 1812, declared by the United States on Britain and its allies. Impressment forced the United States to become entangled with foreign affairs and war.
