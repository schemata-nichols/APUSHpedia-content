> Mercantilism was basically a global economic strategy where European empires competed for world power by using their American colonies to build up as much wealth and control over trade as possible.

## Section 1

Back then, European nations thought the total amount of money and wealth in the world was like a fixed pie, so you could only get richer by taking gold and silver away from your rivals. To pull this off, Britain tried to get a favorable balance of trade, which just means they wanted to sell way more stuff to other places than they actually bought from them. They forced the Thirteen Colonies to help by shipping over cheap raw materials like tobacco and sugar to Britain, and then forced the colonists to buy back the finished goods made from those materials. Parliament made laws called the Navigation Acts to control where colonial ships could go, but they barely even enforced them because of a policy called salutary neglect. once Britain finally started enforcing all these trade rules and taxes after 1763, the colonists got super mad, which pretty much kicked off the American Revolution. 

## Section 2

AI revised version: European nations believed that global wealth was like a fixed pie, meaning a country could only gain power by taking gold and silver away from its rivals. To make this work, Britain wanted a favorable balance of trade by selling way more goods to other countries than it bought from them. The Thirteen Colonies were forced to play a major role in this system by shipping cheap raw materials like tobacco, timber, and sugar back to Britain, and then being forced to buy back expensive British manufactured items. Parliament even passed laws like the Navigation Acts to strictly control colonial trade routes, but they didn't really enforce them at first during a period known as salutary neglect. Once Britain finally cracked down and started strictly enforcing these mercantilist rules and taxes after 1763, the colonists got super angry, which eventually helped spark the American Revolution.
