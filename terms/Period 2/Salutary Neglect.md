## Section 1

Salutary Neglect was Britain's unofficial policy of loosely enforcing trade regulations and other laws in its American colonies during the 1700s. Although Britain technically controlled colonial trade through laws like the navigation acts, often times these laws were not strictly enforced. This allows the colonies to develop a degree of economic independence and practice self-governance through their colonial assemblies.
Salutary Neglect came to a slow end after the French and Indian War, when Britain sought to raise revenue and strengthen its control over the American colonies. The British Government began to enforce more trade laws and stricter taxes such as The Stamp Act of 1765. Colonists viewed this as a threat to their rights because they had become accustomed to managing their own trade.
