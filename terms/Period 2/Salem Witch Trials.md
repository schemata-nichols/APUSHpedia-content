> The Salem Witch Trials was a cultural trend that went on from June 1692 to May 1693 and was a series of prosecutions toward women who were deemed “witches” by their communities through Salem Village, which was located in Massachusetts Bay Colony. However, the SAlme Witch Trails didn't begin in America but actually started in Switzerland, Germany, France, Low Countries, and Italy in the early 14th century. There were countless witch hunts that resulted in around 110,000 people tried to witchcraft and 40,000 to 60,000 “witches” were executed.
> Witches were described as worshippers of Satan and were using dark magic to accomplish the tasks that Satan wanted them to do, they were also seen as shapeshifters and would change from an animal to a human to disguise themselves in the village. Once a “witch” was discovered through rumors and suspicions they would be tried before they could define themselves and shortly after executed.
> Although these trials were ongoing, a man by the name of Samuel Parris, a merchant from Boston and studied theological studies at modern day Harvard University, brought his family to Salem Village. Throughout their time, Parris’s daughter and niece started to act out and become increasingly strange, when the local doctor came he identified it as “supernatural”, even though it could be something completely different such as asthma, Lyme disease, epilepsy, or child abuse. However that sort of healthcare didn't really exist back then, so the doctor recommended a “witch cake” where the victim’s urine is an ingredient in this cake. This angered Parris and caused him to ask the girls for answers who then put the blame on a woman named TItuba, who alter confessed to making a deal with the devil and giving up the souls of the girls.The accusation got worse and worse and another family, the Putmans, started to abuse their power when they came to Salem. They made a deal with the colonists to tell the court if they were a witch and they wouldn't be in trouble but it still led to many accusations amongst many people and they later apologized for abusing their power and the Salem Witch Trials.  This allowed the Salem Witch Trials to officially die down and thus live forever in the history books.

## Section 1

The Salem Witch Trials reveal how gender roles , social hierarchies, religion, and colonial governance, Puritanism, and theocracy through Period 2 shaped all the British colonies throughout America. Furthermore, the spread of Puritanism is a leading role throughout the Salem witch trials because if anyone even acts slightly off or did something merely bad then they would be deemed a witch and later be executed. Puritans also took over Salem Village for some time and led all the colonists to believe more and more that there were really witches sent by Satan to cast dark magic. The idea of law was also very corrupted throughout this time since the victim didn't have too big of a say against the accusations, once someone has an accusation then they would be immediately tried without a second thought. There was furthermore a larger amount of Theocracy and Religious conformity due to the implementation of religion in the governance of this village, which was also led by people who were priests and weren't very literate when it came to the difference between a disease and being a witch. The Salem WItch Trials overall describe the colonies and highlight the amount of gender, social, and religious hierarchy that colonies like Massachusetts had and people who did not follow those criteria were prosecuted and treated horribly.
