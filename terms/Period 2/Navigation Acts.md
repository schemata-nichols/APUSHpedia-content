> A series of laws passed by Britain that regulated trade from British colonies, ensuring each colony's trade would benefit Britain economically.

## Section 1

The Navigation acts are a classic example of mercantilist policies that were used by Britain in America before independence, in order to ensure trade from American colonies benefitted mainland Britain. Enumerated goods( tobacco, sugar, cotton, indigo) could only be sold to the English Empire, ensuring trade and goods flowed within the British colonial system.

## Section 2

However, this was not one-sided for English colonies. Imported goods from other European colonies had import taxes and were cheaper than goods that came from within the empire. Goods produced within the empire naturally became more popular than those bought from outside the colony system.
