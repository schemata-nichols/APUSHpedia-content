## Section 1

Chattel slavery was the race-based system that made it possible for slaves to be inherited through generations of owners and legally considered their personal property. Slaves' children were also the property of their parents' owners. This system stripped slaves' rights down to livestock and goods within the law.

## Section 2

Chattel slavery first became well known within the United States during the African slave trade, which forcibly moved millions of African people from their home country to all across North and South America to work on plantations and in other forms of hard labor. Due to its system being primarily based on racial aspects, Chattel Slavery caused a century-long racial discrimination against people of color that is still evident in today's society.

## Section 3

It was most common in the American South, where plantations, usually growing tobacco and cotton, required hard, brutal labor. Many slave owners were abusive and cruel to their slaves, using whips and other extreme punishments to force faster, more profitable work. It wasn't until the American Civil War that emancipation, with Abraham Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation, was enforced nationwide, and slavery was made illegal in the US.
