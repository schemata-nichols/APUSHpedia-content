## Section 1

Indentured servitude refers to when people who lived in Europe and other areas of the Old World were able to come to the Americas by agreeing to work for a farmer or planter for a couple of years in exchange for their trip to America being payed for. In addition, indentured servants usually received a small amount of land once they became free so that they could start their own farm and make money. However, conditions were not always healthy for indentured servants, they had to perform hard labor with harsh working conditions. Also, indentured servants could be bought or sold and had to follow orders from their employers.  In addition to this, sometimes the amount of land the indentured servants received once becoming free was not enough to help them get started, and many had to go back to low, unskilled jobs. This was mainly because rich planters had taken up most of the land, and there was not a lot of land left in the boundary of the colonies for the indentured servants. Also, the government did not want to expand out west because they feared conflict with the Native Americans who lived there. The resentment of the indentured servants led to Bacon's Rebellion in 1676.
