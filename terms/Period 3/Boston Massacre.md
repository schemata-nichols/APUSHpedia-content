> In 1770, Great Britain sent troops to Boston to protect officials trying to enforce British laws and taxes. A crowd of colonists began to harass British soldiers, who opened fire on the crowd. Five colonists were killed in the incident, which became a turning point in the growing conflict between Britain and the colonies.

## Section 1

The Boston Massacre occurred on March 5, 1770. At the time, many colonists were angry with Britain because of the taxes they had to pay and the British soldiers living in their city. The colonists thought the taxes were unfair because they did not have a say in the British government. This caused many arguments and tension between the colonists and the soldiers.

## Section 2

On the night of the Boston Massacre, a crowd of colonists began arguing with British soldiers. The crowd threw snowballs, rocks, and other objects at the soldiers. Things got out of control, and the soldiers fired their guns into the crowd. Five colonists were killed, and several other people were injured during the incident.

## Section 3

The Boston Massacre was an important event that helped lead to the American Revolution. After it happened, many colonists became even angrier with Britain. Paul Revere made an engraving of the event titled *The Bloody Massacre Perpetrated in King-Street Boston on March 5th 1770 by a Party of the 29th Reg*. The engraving showed British soldiers firing at colonists, making the soldiers appear to be the enemy. Although the image did not show every detail of what happened accurately, it helped spread anger toward Britain and encouraged opposition to British rule.

## Section 4

Even though the event took place over a short period, it increased tensions between the colonists and Britain and helped bring the colonists closer to fighting for independence.
