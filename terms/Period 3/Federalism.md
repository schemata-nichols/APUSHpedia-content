> A system of government where power is split between the national government and the state governments. It was created in the Constitution after the Articles of Confederation proved too weak. The national government got powers like taxing and regulating trade, while states kept other powers.

## Section 1

Federalism came out of the problems of the 1780s. Under the Articles of Confederation, the national government was very weak. It couldn't tax, it couldn't control trade between states, and it had trouble handling problems like Shay's Rebellion. At the Constitutional Convention, leaders wanted a stronger national government, but many Americans still feared too much power in one place after fighting a king. Federalism was the compromise, power was shared between the national government and the states. The Constitution gave the national government powers like collecting taxes, running the military, and controlling trade. States kept powers over things like local laws and education, splitting the national government into three branches with checks and balances. The people who supported this were called Federalists, like James Madison and Alexander Hamilton, they argued for this system in the Federalist Papers. The people who were opposed to these ideals were called Anti-Federalists, they worried it gave the national government too much power and demanded a Bill of Rights. The balance between national and state power became a major debate moving forward in American History.
