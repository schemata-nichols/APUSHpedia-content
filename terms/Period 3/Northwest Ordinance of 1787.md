## Submission by Leonardo Kelly

> The Northwest Ordinance was passed by Congress under the Articles of Confederation in 1787. It established a process for territories to become states, protected certain rights, encouraged education, and banned slavery in the Northwest Territory. It helped the United States expand westward and establish rules for new states.

### Section 1

The Northwest Ordinance was passed in 1787 under the Articles of Confederation. It established a process for western territories to become states equal to the original states. It also banned slavery in the Northwest Territory and encouraged public education. The law helped the United States expand westward and maintain order in its new territories.

## Submission by Eva Robinson

> The Northwest Ordinance of 1787 was the last of three ordinances set in place by Congress in the 1780s in order to establish how to divide the western land acquired from expansion. In 1787, the ordinance enacted called for the creation of the Northwest territory (three to five states north of the Ohio River and east of Mississippi) leaving the area with conditions for self-government and statehood. Additionally, each state obtained its own Bill of Rights. These three ordinances were the basis for what President Jefferson labeled the empire of liberty. Regarding this principle, the ordinance also prohibited enslavement in the old Northwest.

### Section 1

APUSH Significance
The ordinance established a three-step governance system that permitted territories to become equal states on the condition that they reached a population of 60,000 free inhabitants. Furthermore, the ordinance ensured that the states could develop as equal, established nations with no one power having control over a state.

### Section 2

Expansion and Contradiction
Although the law promised Native land would be left alone, this ordinance directly contradicted that sentiment because the new land that the Northwest occupied was previously inhabited by farms and towns of Native Shawnees, Delawares, and their allies. The ordinance set the foundation for rapid white settlement ultimately objectifying the Native peoples and communities around them.
