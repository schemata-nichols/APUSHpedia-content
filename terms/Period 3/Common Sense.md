> Common Sense was a pamphlet written by Thomas Paine in 1776 to support American independence. Paine had immigrated from England in 1774, offering a unique perspective to advocate for independence from the British empire. Published anonymously, Paine condemned the British ideologies of a monarch government and hereditary rule. Paine explained that becoming independent from the British empire was in the best interests of the colonists, promoting self-government. Paine did not believe the British island should rule the American continent. The document had an alternative style to other publications of its time, written for ordinary people with clear and direct language, rather than targeted to the elite. The document was important for gaining rapid, popular support for colonial independence.

## Section 1

Common Sense was an extremely influential document in American history, inspiring a popular movement for separation from the British by the colonists, only months after its release. Colonists were not just complaining about the British and resisting their policies but became motivated to advocate for independence as a nation. It built popular support for self-government in the months leading up to the Declaration of Independence. The document demonstrated the power of the written press to engage public opinion, with relatable language that the masses could appreciate. The pamphlet helped gain support for a representative government rather than monarchal rule, which became some of the basic ideals of the initial U.S. government.
