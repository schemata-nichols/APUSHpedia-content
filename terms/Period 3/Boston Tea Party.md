## Submission by Milan Travers

> The Boston tea party was a political protest on December 16, 1773, when colonists protested taxation without representation by dumping 342 chests of British tea into the Boston Harbor. The protest was organized by a protest group known as the Son of Liberty in response to the Tea Act, which gave the British East India Company an advantage in the tea selling business. Britain retaliated against this protest with the Intolerable Acts, which restricted Massachusetts political power and self government while also closing the Boston Harbor, thus weakening the economy. These events helped rally support for the American Revolution.

### Section 1

The Boston Tea Party was a grand act of colonial resistance that took place on December 16, 1773 in Boston, Massachusetts. It was caused due to tensions between Britain and the American colonies after the French and Indian war, which caused Britain to have a lot of debt. To pay this debt off, Britain imposed taxes and trade restrictions like the Stamp Act and Townshend Acts. A major reason colonists disliked these acts was because they had no say/representation in Parliament to refute them.
Despite Parliament repealing many of these Taxes, it still passed the TeaAct of 1773 to help the British East India Company. The act let the company sell tea directly to the colonies which gave it a huge advantage over colonial merchants. Even though the Tea Act lowered the price of Tea for colonists, many opposed it because they felt it Britain was using it to establish taxation authority over the colonies. Protests spread throughout the colonies, and several smaller acts involving tea such as preventing it from being unloaded started occuring. In Boston, however, Massachusetts Governer Thomas Hutchinson insisted that the tea ships parked there remain in the harbor until the duties were paid and the tea was unloaded.
On the night of December 16, a group of colonists under the Sons of Liberty, including some men disguised as Native Americans, boarded three ships; the Dartmouth, the Eleanor, and the Beaver. They then broke open and dumped 342 chests of tea  into the Boston Harbor. This protest was organized to prevent the tea from entering Massachusetts and to reject Parliaments attempt at the authority to tax the colonies without their input or consent. The participants in the protest were careful to not damage anything other than the tea to send the message to Parliament.
Britain responded in a fury in 1774 with the Coercive Acts, known by the colonists as the Intolerable Acts. These acts closed Boston Harbor until the destroyed tea's price came out of Massachusetts wallet, restricted Massachusetts self governing abilities, and increased British presence and control over the colonies. These acts then led to the First Continental Congress, which included representative from 12 colonies, who then discussed their coordinated response to the new British policies.
The Boston Tea Party was a crucial turning point in the growing conflict between Britain and its American colonies. It intensified tensions, strengthened colonial unity, and also set the stage for the American Revolution.

## Submission by Jack Berndt

> American colonists were angered about the Tea Act of 1773, which taxed imported tea and gave an unfair monopoly to the British East India Company. As a result of this, the American revolutionary group called the Sons of Liberty (led by New England political figures like Sam Adams) secretly boarded three ships docked in the Boston Harbor, each carrying hundreds of chests of tea. Disguised as Native Americans, the Sons of Liberty dumped 342 chests of tea into the Boston Harbor, which was worth about $1 million in today's currency.
