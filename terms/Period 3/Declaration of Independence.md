## The Declaration of Independence
> The Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776. It was a formal document declaring that the 13 North American British colonies were no longer under British rule, which was primarily written by Thomas Jefferson and heavily influenced by Enlightenment thinkers such as John Locke and Rousseau.

## Foundation
 The foundation of the document is based on Enlightenment ideals, such as that all people have natural rights (life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness), that governments get power from the consent of the governed, and that the people can alter or abolish unjust government; this power originates with the people, not a monarch, and the citizens have the right to overthrow the government if natural rights are violated. 

## Structure 
The Declaration of Independence is split up into three parts: the first part is the Preamble, which asserts the rights of the individuals; the second part is the grievances, which lists grievances and unfair actions of King George III and Parliament because the colonists were being stripped of their rights as English Citizens; and the final part is the formal Declaration of Independence, which identifies that the 13 colonies are free and independent states, are no longer tied to the British government, and can do what is necessary to govern themselves. 

## Conclusion
In conclusion, the Declaration of Independence helped justify the American Revolution and established ideals about freedom and equality with the claim that “all men are created equal.” This proceeded to influence several movements for women’s rights and African American emancipation.
