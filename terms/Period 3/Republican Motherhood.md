## Revised Entry

Republican Motherhood developed out of the post-revolutionary state of America. As Americans attempted to build a new government, a growing sentiment surrounding one's civic duty became more significant. This appeared in the belief that mothers must teach their sons values of liberty, democracy, and responsibility. It was seen as necessary for the new republic’s citizens to be virtuous and intelligent. A mother’s dedication to her household responsibilities were seen as a service to the nation. This role ultimately required that women receive greater educational opportunities in order to sufficiently teach their children, resulting in the creation of new female academies and higher female student populations. Although women’s role in political spaces was still low. Republican Motherhood restrained women to the household, and their education was exclusively used for teaching children, being unrecognized for political participation. Despite this, the practice still effectively laid the framework for future progress in women’s rights.

## Submission by Mia Reich

> After the American Revolution, people believed a republic could only survive if its citizens put the common good first. Since women couldn't vote or hold office, they were given a different job, raising smart sons who would become good citizens. Many women argued that mothers needed a real education to do this, which led to more schools for girls. It gave women more respect, but it still kept them in the home with no legal or political equality.

### Section 1

Republican Motherhood came out of the American Revolution, when Americans were figuring out how to build a government without a king. They believed a republic could only work if citizens were virtuous, meaning they cared more about the good of the country rather than themselves. Leaders thought this had to be taught, and since mothers raised the children, women were given the job of teaching it. This meant a mother’s everyday work, like raising children and running a household, was seen as a service to the nation. Women still couldn’t vote or hold office, but this idea gave them a political purpose inside the home.
The biggest effect was that more women were attending school. Female academies opened and more women learned to read and write. But, Republican Motherhood did not make women equal to men. It kept them in the home, where men worked outside the home and women ran the household. Women’s education was valued mainly for what it could do for their children and husbands, not for their own independence. In this way, an idea meant to keep women at home helped lay the groundwork for their later role in politics and the fight for women’s rights.

## Submission by Ronak Dhiman

> Republican Motherhood was a body of cultural ideals which dictated how women where to primarily be during the period of the American Revolution and afterward (3rd period) before it switched to the Cult of Domesticity. These ideals where more traditional to the established gender roles of this period and before and had similar structure due to the lack of suffrage granted despite their efforts during the war. Specifically, it dictated that primarily women where responsible of sticking to their homes and raising good or virtuous citizens which will actively participate for the betterment of the United States. In the end, though, it granted some rights to women not seen before like education.

### Section 1

Republican Motherhood was heavily apparent in the period following the American revolution during the transition of the Americas to a more independent colony. Following the industrial revolution, which worked to effectively equalize gender roles temporarily with women working outside as men went to war, the Americas shifted back into a more traditional look on the gender roles like before. This went against much efforts of women to contradict the established gender roles, but still worked to provide some new liberties to them. From these events it paved republican motherhood to be primarily about raising and educating the next generation of children to be virtuous citizens.

### Section 2

Due to republican motherhood having a huge emphasis on the countries future and instilling proper habits it also reflected onto women. This included increased rights and benefits they previously haven't seen before like the increased rights to education. The state, wanting an educated future, emphasized women's education more so under these ideals as without the proper education women weren't getting they otherwise couldn't fulfill the goals of the idea. This overall led to more women being educated than before, where it was mainly only men, so they could raise the next generation and improve their opportunities in these intellectual ways. Out of more general reforms and rights this was the most emphasized one.

### Section 3

This ideology slowly started to fall out of the public sphere to be replaced by the cult of domesticity as the Americas started to get more established. It was mainly so apparent during this period only as the country was widely new and needed to have a secure future which would help run the country. this concern dwindled down as more generations grew up successfully.

## Submission by Bedie Rittling

> Republican Motherhood was the belief that women should raise and teach their children to be good citizens. It became popular after the American Revolution and helped women gain more education, but they still had few political rights.

### Section 1

Republican Motherhood was the belief that women should raise and educate their children to become responsible citizens who supported democracy and republican values. This idea became popular after the American Revolution because the new country needed educated citizens to keep its government strong. Women were encouraged to receive more education so they could teach their children about freedom, patriotism, and responsibility. However, women still had limited rights because they were expected to focus on their families rather than participate in government. Republican Motherhood helped improve women's education but did not give them political equality.

## Submission by Kaylie Johnson

> Republican Motherhood was the 18th century American ideal that women should be educated to be able to properly raise and educate their sons to become politically-conscious citizens. Women still weren’t allowed to vote or hold parliament, but were allowed to go to school. These ideals became used for arguments against women’s suffrage.

### Section 1

In the 18th century, during American Revolutionary ideals, came women challenging the previously cherished women’s roles in the West. Women were using their voices politically, working on farms, and even attempting to join the army. However, the Revolution failed to deliver a lasting change in women’s social status in America. Instead, after the Revolution, ideals began to shift for the “ultimate good of the people”, in which women, specifically white women, were allowed to be educated, but for the reason that they were to be put into domestic roles to teach their sons to be more “good” and politically-conscious. Women were still not allowed to vote or be held a place in parliament.

### Section 2

Although women’s rights were restricted, the education of women was a benefit from Republican Motherhood. Though women’s education was less prevalent than men, women’s education allowed women to be able to know how to be politically active, eventually leading to women’s active roles in social and political movements in the 19th century, such as women’s suffrage.
