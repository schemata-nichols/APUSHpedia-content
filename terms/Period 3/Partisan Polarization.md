> Partisan polarization is when political parties become divided because they disagree on government policies. During the Election of 1800, Thomas Jefferson’s Democratic-Republicans and John Adams’s Federalists disagreed over federal power and the economy. The issue of partisan polarization was previously discussed by Washington in his farewell address of 1796 where he asserted that differing political parties would divide the nation and make it more vulnerable to foreign entanglements. Additionally, he condemned partisan polarization because he believed it had the ability to distract and weaken public councils and administrations and could ultimately lead to riots and discord among the nation.

## Section 1

Partisan polarization increased in the 1790s as Federalists and Democratic-Republicans disagreed over the power of the federal government, the national bank, and the economy. These disagreements led to political conflict during the Election of 1800 between John Adams and Thomas Jefferson. Jefferson won the election, showing a peaceful transfer of power between opposing political parties.
