> Shays’ Rebellion (1786–1787) was an armed uprising in Massachusetts led by Daniel Shays, a former Revolutionary War soldier. Following the American Revolution, many farmers faced severe economic difficulties due to debt, high taxes, and a shortage of hard currency. Unable to pay their debts and taxes, some farmers risked losing their land and property. Frustrated with the Massachusetts government’s policies and lack of economic relief, Shays and other farmers organized protests and attempted to shut down courts to prevent foreclosures and debt collections. The uprising eventually escalated into an armed rebellion, which was suppressed by a privately funded militia.

## Section 1

Shays’ Rebellion demonstrated the weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation, particularly the national government’s inability to raise taxes, maintain a strong military, and effectively respond to domestic unrest. Although the rebellion was suppressed by a state militia rather than the national government, it alarmed many political leaders who feared that the government could not maintain order or protect economic stability. As a result, the rebellion strengthened calls for a more powerful central government and helped encourage the Constitutional Convention of 1787, where delegates drafted the U.S. Constitution. In APUSH, Shays’ Rebellion is significant because it illustrates how economic instability and the weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation contributed to the movement for a stronger federal government.
