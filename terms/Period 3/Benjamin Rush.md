## Section 1

Benjamin Rush (1746-1838) was an American physician, educator, social reformer, and Founding Father. He studied medicine at the University of Edinburgh and later became a professor at the College of Philadelphia, which eventually became the University of Pennsylvania. During the American Revolution, he served as a surgeon general in the Continental Army. After the war, Rush advocated for abolitionism, prison reform, temperance, and improved public education, including better opportunities for women. He helped establish Dickinson College and the Philadelphia Dispensary, which provided medical care to people who could not afford it. He also contributed to the early study of mental illness. His work helped shape early American medicine and social reform.
