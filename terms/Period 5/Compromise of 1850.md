> The Compromise of 1850 was a series of five bills meant to solve the tension between the North and the South following the Mexican-American War. It admitted California as a free state, permitted slavery in Washington D.C. but banned the slave trade, helped resolve the Texas border, allowed Utah and New Mexico to decide if they’d be a free or a slave state based on popular sovereignty, and made it easier for slave-owners to retrieve fugitive slaves under the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850. This temporarily prevented the Civil War.

## Section 1

Senator Henry Clay, a member of the Whig Party, decided to come up with the Compromise of 1850 in fear of the growing divide between the North and South United States eventually leading to a Civil War. He presented five different bills to the Senate, that California would join the Union as a free state, that Washington D.C. is permitted to have slaves, but the slave trade must be outlawed, defined new boundaries for Texas following the Mexican-American War, allowed New Mexico and Utah to decide whether or not they should allow slaves based on popular sovereignty, and creating the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, making it so fugitive slaves would be easier to be retrieved, and that the people had a duty to participate in returning fugitive slaves. The full compromise was unable to pass, so the bills became split up individually, which led to them being passed.

## Section 2

The Compromise of 1850 temporarily postponed the Civil War, however, not for long. The Compromise enraged Northern Abolitionists due to them having a duty to return fugitive slaves, and ended up with the Act fueling the Underground Railroad. The Compromise also made some Southerners question whether the Union was even worth maintaining.
