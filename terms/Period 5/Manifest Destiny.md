> Manifest Destiny is an expansionist cultural mindset that the people of the United States are destined by God to expand westward toward the Pacific Ocean. This 19th century belief was fostered by the ideas of spreading democracy, the “American way of life”, the superiority of the "Anglo-Saxon race", and was used to justify the removal of Native Americans. The westward expansion brought up the issue of slavery in states and was a key proponent of the Civil War.

## Section 1

Content:
Mexican Cession:
In 1821, Mexico gained independence from Spain but the northern provinces of New Mexico, California, and Texas were isolated. The population of these territories were largely Native American and were sparsely settled by non-Native Americans of Spanish origin (Tejanos). In 1820, a farmer from Connecticut named Moses Austin made an offer to the Spanish government to help colonize the land with American settlers for development. In the 1830s the Mexican government believed that it was losing control over the area and barred emigration from the United States.

## Section 2

Texas Revolt:
American settlers and the Tejano elites wanted greater autonomy with the Mexican government so they held a revolt led by Stephen Austin because the inhabitants of the area favored the economic boom that came with the introduction of the Americans. An army was sent by General Antonio Lopez de Santa Anna in 1835 to impose authority but ended up sparking the Texas Revolt. The rebels demanded Texan independence and even formed a provisional government. In 1836, the army stormed a mission compound named the Alamo and killed many Americans and Tejanos. However, under San Housten, the rebels forced Santa Anna’s army to recognize Texan independence. The Texan Congress wanted Texas to join the United States but 1837 President Martin Van Buren deferred that offer because of the controversy over whether Texas should join as a slave state.

## Section 3

Texas, Oregon, and California Under Polk:
In 1845, James K. Polk was inaugurated as President with a platform that supported the annexation of Texas and the occupation of Oregon and right before he started in office congress voted Texas part of the United States. Polk sent an emissary to the Mexican government to negotiate the purchase of California but was refused and by 1846, was arranging military action.

## Section 4

Mexican War:
The Mexican War was fought to gain control of California and New Mexico and was the first time American troops occupied a foreign capital. 60,000 Americans banded together to fight the war and in July of 1846 the U.S. Navy sailed into the harbors of Monterey and San Antonio. Under Winfield Scott American forces marched into Mexican City and occupied the capital. The Mexican and American government agreed to the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848 affirming the annexation of Texas and the cession of New Mexico, California, Arizona, Nevada and Utah all to the United States. Mexico lost one-third of its territory and split a united territory and divided families. America claimed the annexation of Mexico’s northern territories was an act of salvation because Mexico could not control its northern territories.

## Section 5

Opposition:
Many northerners did not like the Mexican conflict because they believed that instead of just expanding the empire that the American government was acquiring new land for the expansion of slavery. Abraham Lincoln was also a dissenter of the Mexican War not fully believing Polk’s claim that Mexicans had killed people on American land.

## Section 6

Manifest Destiny in Late 1830s and the 1840s:
After Texan independence was achieved, tension grew between Anglos (white settlers) and Tejanos. The superiority of the “Anglo-Saxon” race was a major facet of the idea of manifest destiny. This came to light when white settlers turned on tejanos because they suspected them of loyalty to Mexico, made them out to be foreigners, pulled them from leadership positions, and limited them to unskilled labor. Furthermore, in 1848 gold was discovered at a sawmill in the foothills of the Sierra Nevada mountains in California. Hundreds of thousands of people came rushing to California in search of gold in what was called the Gold Rush. People came from Mexico, South America, Ireland, Germany, Italy, China, Australia, and the Eastern United States. Ethnic conflicts arose with white miners evicting “foreign miners”, maintaining prejudice, and giving low wages. Native Americans were also overrun by miners, they were attacked by militias launched by state officials, and many Native American children were sold as slaves, all dwindling California's Indigenous population.
