> The Battle of Antietam took place on September 17, 1862, at Antietam Creek in western Maryland, close to the Potomac River and the Virginia border. The conflict was an important battle of the Civil War, often considered a major turning point and remains as the deadliest day in American military history. The battle involved Robert E. Lee's Confederate troops pushing north into Maryland to confront the Union's Army of the Potomac under the command of George B. McClellan. After much bloodshed, the battle was fought to a standoff. However, the confrontation was considered a strategic victory for the Union, forcing Lee's troops to retreat, holding off his advances toward the North, and dampening the momentum of the Confederate military campaign in the North.

## Section 1

In addition to being one of the bloodiest days in American history, the Battle of Antietam was a major turning point in the Civil War. The Union's success at this battle, by stopping the advance of the Confederate troops into the North, gave Lincoln the military accomplishment he had been waiting to achieve. The strategic victory provided him an opportunity to finally announce his preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, which he did five days after the Battle of Antietam. This expanded the goal of the war from not just preserving the Union but also ending slavery. Up until this point, Great Britain and France were considering recognition of the Confederacy and intervention to help mediate an end to the war. The Union's success at this battle and the subsequent release of the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation helped strengthen the Union's international position, discouraging Britain and France from taking a stronger stance to recognize the Confederacy.
