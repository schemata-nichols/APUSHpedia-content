Cash crops are crops which serve no upfront practical purposes like food, but are rather fashioned by groups like European settlers or other groups for other purposes. These crops where often harvested by exploited labor in areas like plantations and drove economies based usually on it alone. This led to them serving huge importance overall to a variety of American history as it drove a wide variety of debates and other political issues due to its lucrativeness. Out of most of the cash crops two are crucial in understanding to derive the mindsets of many Americans in their respective periods. This includes Tobacco from period one and cotton from periods four to five.

### Section 2

Tobacco is native to North America and was the first primary initial cash crop for colonizers when they first started to colonize into North America during the Age of Exploration (period 1). Many European countries sought tobacco as medicine due to its ability to calm the mind from nicotine. But, more prominently, it was later more sought out for its recreational and addictive properties from the nicotine inside of the plant. Due to this massive demand many turned to labor of enslaved Native American's during this time primarily in areas like New Spain to fuel it and turn around a huge profit. This led to uprisings and rebellions due to the forced labor and cruelty often exhibited over trying to meet demand for this one crop. Though, in all, this set a basis for the more prominent slave labors seen in the future and overall shows the general "White Man" mindset towards cash crops like this.

Though, there's one invention called the cotton gin by Eli Whitney which made it marginally easier to cultivate cotton due to its streamline of removing seeds from the cotton. This invention caused a huge boom in slave labor in plantations to fuel the demand for cotton as it was widely easier to produce way more cotton using it especially with "free" labor. The south grew into a massive cotton kingdom which relied on active slave labor of primarily African Americans to get as much profit as they could as fast as they could like tobacco. ## Submission by Oliver Ptak

> A Cash Crop is a crop that is intended to be sold for a profit, instead of being grown to feed the farmer and their family. A few examples of cash crops in colonial America, and the Antebellum South are tobacco, indigo, and cotton.

### Section 1

Cash crops helped colonial economies grow and increased the use of plantations for agricultural production. As plantations expanded the production of crops such as tobacco, sugar, and cotton, they needed more enslaved Africans to provide forced labor. This growing demand created and contributed to the expansion of the transatlantic slave trade, which took more than 10 million Africans to the Americas. The labor of enslaved Africans supported the growth of plantation agriculture, while also being the cause of human suffering as well as social and economic consequences.
