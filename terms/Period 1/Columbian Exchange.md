## Submission by Alexis Miyashita

> The Columbian Exchange was when goods traveled from the old world to the new world and viseversa, this caused for the old world to flourish by living longer and by being able to produce more crops to feed themselves. Although this was a positive experience for people in the old world, people in the new world struggled to surrvive because of the diseases that were brought over.

### Section 1

The Columbian Exchange was the trading of goods to/from the old and new world after Christopher Columus' voyage to the new world. This exchange gave the old world things such as new cash crops e.g. Corn, Potatoes, sweet potatoes, cassava, and etc; new animals such as turkeys and llamas. The new food brought the life expectancy of people from the old world up exponentially. The new world got animals such as horses, cattle, pigs, chickens, and etc; they also got crops such as wheat, barley, oats, and etc; although they got many goods from this trade there were also many diseases that came to the new world and killed many Native Americans such as smallpox, measles, influenza, and etc.

## Submission by Levi Mayer

> The Columbian Exchange was the cross-Atlantic trade between the Old world and New World. The exchange of goods, people, animals, plants, religion, inventions, and disease had immense impacts on both the inhabitants of and landscape of the Old and New Worlds. Raw materials were harvested and brought from the Americas, then from Europe the materials were used in manufacturing and brought again to the Americas. Europe also used materials from the Americas to continue trade with Africa and Asia for luxury goods. Livestock was brought to the Americas and different agriculture techniques were introduced. Diseases were also introduced to the Indigenous populations severely impacting their communities.

### Section 1

The Columbian Exchange impacted many elements of the continents it touched, and can almost be described as global trade. Diets became more enriched, thus supporting population growth. The introduction of livestock changed the landscape of the Americas, and the search for silver that was encouraged by the trade was a catalyst for the slavery of indigenous populations in the Americas. The diseases brought over also wiped out about ninety percent of indigenous populations, leaving them susceptible to colonization. The Columbian Exchange eventually led to the Transatlantic Slave Trade, when native communities were too small colonists wanted an easy source of free labor.
