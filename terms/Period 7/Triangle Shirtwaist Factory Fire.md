> On March 25th, 1911, one of the most influential industrial buildings was burnt thus killing 146 workers. The  name of this building was the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory which was a sweatshop in NYC that was known for having bad factory conditions and even worse safety conditions, the conditions were so horrible that it led to the death of the workers and or caused them to be extremely ill. The factory would usually employ immigrant women or underaged girls to work at the factory, for 8 hours a day they were in lines, sewing piece by piece with little to no breaks, the elevators rarely functioned and were usually inaccessible and the fire exit was so narrow that it would take hours for everyone to get out successfully. This factory was owned by two men, Max Blanck and Isaac Harris, who were extremely abusive toward their workers, paying them $9-$15 a week despite them working everyday for 8-12 hours. After the first time the factory burnt down the owners refused to put in water sprinklers which was later a huge issue.
> 	On March 25th, the second fire had erupted, due to the nonexistent water sprinkler the manager of the factory was attempting to use the fire hose but was unsuccessful, with 600 workers in one building with just one exit, everyone was rushing to get out, thus leading to the collapse of the stairwell, smoke suffocating people,and even workers jumping out of windows. The fire kept spreading and spreading and a total of 146 people were killed before the firefighters could even get a handle on the situation.
> 	After the fire, the communities came together to stand against the prejudice that all these workers face, however even with all the evidence that the owners were neglectful and horrible the prosecution failed to convict them successfully. On the other hand after much fighting from the community against President Franklin D. Roosevelt and the senators, it created the Sullivan-Hoey Fire Prevention LAw which passed that October.
